The question behind HAA

When a housing system is required to perform or document an activity, can independent evidence verify that it occurred?

Why this work exists

Housing systems should be understandable from the record they create.

Publicly funded housing programs often operate through networks of agencies, contractors, providers, property operators, case managers, and oversight bodies. When responsibility is fragmented, participants may be left trying to reconstruct decisions, deadlines, services, and outcomes themselves.

HAA addresses that information imbalance by organizing the administrative record, identifying the standards that apply, and distinguishing what can be verified from what remains unanswered.

What HAA does

Evidence first. Conclusions second.

Methodology

The archive shows its work.

HAA does not treat an allegation, a record, and a verified finding as the same thing. Its methodology is designed to preserve that distinction.

A

Start with the standard

Identify the governing authority, administrative requirement, or published procedure before evaluating what the record shows.

B

Identify the expected record

Determine what documentation should reasonably exist if the required activity occurred.

C

Classify what can be verified

Use defined verification statuses rather than filling evidentiary gaps with assumptions.

D

Preserve correction and response

Maintain source boundaries, correction procedures, and an opportunity for affected entities to provide responsive records or context.
