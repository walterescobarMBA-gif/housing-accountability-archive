Housing Accountability Archive offers specialized research and documentation technical assistance for organizations administering publicly funded housing programs, with an initial focus on HOPWA.

Discuss an engagement
Our central question
When program requirements call for documentation, can the organization demonstrate what occurred—and what happened to the participant?

Our method connects program requirements to available administrative records, identifies what can and cannot be verified, and translates documented gaps into practical process-improvement recommendations.

Core capabilities

One integrated documentation-to-improvement workflow

01 / VERIFY

Documentation verification

Compare applicable requirements with authorized program records. Distinguish documented actions from missing, inconsistent, or unresolved evidence.

02 / CONTINUITY

Housing continuity review

Examine how participant status, referrals, service delivery, provider transitions, and housing exits are documented.

03 / PREPARE

Monitoring readiness

Organize evidence against an agreed review framework and identify documentation weaknesses that may affect monitoring readiness.

04 / IMPROVE

Process improvement

Develop practical verification checklists, responsibility matrices, recordkeeping workflows, and action recommendations.

Method and safeguards

Evidence-based. Participant-centered. Clear about limitations.

We identify the governing requirement, the documentation expected, the evidence made available, and the resulting verification status. We do not treat an absent record as conclusive proof that an activity never occurred.

Engagements require a defined scope, conflict screening, confidentiality terms, and appropriate authorization before access to identifiable participant information. De-identified or process-level materials are preferred when feasible.

HAA's independent public-interest publication and paid technical-assistance activities are distinct. Client information is not automatically part of the public archive.
